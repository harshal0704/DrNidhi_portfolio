Abstract

In Vachaspata, the verse reads "pathya-pathe, marge, hite chikitsad-au, hitakarakabhoktryadravyabhedahe" — pathya is that which walks the path of benefit and is wholesome in ahi (food), vihara (conduct) and chikitsa (treatment). The word pathya derives from the root path, literally a way or channel. In Shabdakalpadrum, pathya is described as that which is beneficial to the patient, while apathya harms. Because the characteristics and properties of food change according to season, place and person, pathya must change accordingly. In the Yogaratnakara, the three most important factors in treating disease are etiology, drug treatment and pathya. Judicious planning of treatment by proper understanding of pathya therefore yields a far more effective eradication of disease than medicine alone. This seminar applies the principle of pathya specifically to Prasuti Tantra and Stree Roga, where dietary and behavioural regulation carries unusual weight across conception, pregnancy, labour and the puerperium.
