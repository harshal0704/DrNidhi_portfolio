Can a simple oil-based Uttar Basti measurably restore menstrual flow in arthava kshaya? This randomised clinical study answers the question in 30 women.

Abstract

Artava Kshaya (hypomenorrhoea) is a common menstrual complaint in reproductive-age women and a frequent cause of fertility concern. This randomised clinical study was undertaken in the Department of Prasuti Tantra & Stree Roga, Dr. N.R.S. Govt. Ayurvedic College, Vijayawada, to evaluate the efficacy of Lashuna Kalpa and Rasnadi Taila administered by Uttar Basti in the management of Artava Kshaya w.s.r. to Hypomenorrhoea. A sample of 30 subjects diagnosed with Artava Kshaya was recruited from the outpatient department of Prasuti Tantra and Stree Roga at Achanta Lakshmipathi Ayurvedic Hospital, Vijayawada. Diagnosis followed the classical vyadhi pratyatma lakshana — yathochita kala adarshanam (intermenstrual interval over 35 days), alpartava (reduced quantity of menstrual blood) and yoni vedana (lower abdominal pain) — in women aged 18–40 with scanty bleeding for two to three consecutive months, with anaemia, systemic illness, endocrine disorders and androgen-producing tumours excluded. Intervention combined oral Lashuna Kalpa churna (12 g twice daily with ksheera, ghrita or navaneet) with Rasnadi Taila administered by Uttar Basti. Each subject was assessed on an individual proforma covering history, physical signs and symptoms, dosha and dushya pareeksha, with the findings categorised and scored and analysed statistically. Lashuna Kalpa was prepared as a fine churna of shade-dried clove, and Rasnadi Taila was prepared from the classical kwatha and kalka dravyas — rasna, yashtimadhu, devadaru, malati, bala, chitraka, guduchi, kantakari and yuthika — boiled with jala, gomutra and godugdha in moorchita tila taila to madhyama paka. The findings support the use of Uttar Basti as a safe, low-cost, non-invasive and clinically effective intervention in Artava Kshaya, with significant improvement in the assessed clinical parameters.
