Ayurveda does not have an "immunity" chapter — it has a daily schedule. This seminar shows how the two map onto each other.

Abstract

Dinacharya is defined as the daily routine. According to Ayurveda, one should follow Dinacharya in order to lead a healthy and disease-free life. Ayurveda gives more emphasis on the prevention of disease than on its treatment, and Dinacharya is the practical vehicle for that principle: it establishes balance in one's constitution, regularises the biological clock, aids digestion, absorption and assimilation, and generates self-esteem, discipline, peace, happiness and longevity. This seminar presents Dinacharya as the daily immune strategy — a structured, timed sequence of Ahara, Vihara and Achara that keeps the doshas in balance and maintains the body's natural defence capacity, and contrasts it with modern behaviour-based approaches to immune resilience.

Slide 2 — DINACHARYA

According to Ayurveda one should follow Dinacharya in order to lead a healthy and disease free life.

Ayurveda gives more emphasis on prevention of diseases than treating a disease.

resist external infection.

Ayurveda describes it under

Vyadhikshamatwa , Bala, Agni and Ojus.

Melatonin hormone (mood elevator) is produced much in early morning.

It work as powerful antioxidant, fight against the disease.

It increases stamina, provide stability to body and increases Agni.

Slide 13 — आहार:-

Like this one should take Ahara in a chronological order.

And one should take anupana as jala. Because this Ahara rasa gives strength, bala, varna, pushti, dhatu poshana, indriya prasadata etc.

Fruits, roasted cereals like gram, peanuts, pulses etc. Sprouted cereals, Puffed rice, butter milk are considered as healthy food items.

It is needed to change our style of food as: Consume a diet rich in vegetables and fruits , whole fruit is better than fruit juice because it contains fiber.

Vegetables which are green, reduces fat and blood sugar level.

Prepare food by boiling, roasting, steaming, baking and avoid frying.
