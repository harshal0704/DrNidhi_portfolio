Effect of Pumsavana Karma in Conception

A classical review of the first of the Shodasha Samskaras for Garbhasthireekarana

"Pumsavanam ithi pumsatva karakam karma" — this article explains the Ayurvedic conception-care ritual that literally means "the ritual that brings forth the child".

Abstract

Pumsavana Karma, the first of the Shodasha Samskaras, is performed to secure a healthy, intelligent and prosperous progeny — the classical aim of Suputrakameeya. The karma may be performed immediately prior to conception (Pushyanakshatra) and is directed at Garbhadharana (conception) and Garbhasthireekarana (the settling of the embryo). The 10th–16th day of the menstrual cycle is identified as the optimal window for conception, and Pushya Nakshatra — one of the fourteen Purusha Nakshatras and among the most auspicious for medicinal preparations and therapeutic administration — is the prescribed time for its performance. This article reviews the principles and practice of Pumsavana Karma, examining its rationale, timing, procedure and therapeutic intent, and presents it as a safe, cost-effective and evidence-informed approach to improving conception outcomes. The review positions Pumsavana as a bridge between classical Samskara doctrine and contemporary fertility-seeking practice.
