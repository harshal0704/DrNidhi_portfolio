Role of Dinacharya in the Prevention of Health Hazards of Pollution

A supervised compilation in the Department of Swasthavritta

Every pollutant has an Ayurvedic counter-measure — and most of them are free. This compilation maps daily routine against modern environmental hazards.

Abstract

This supervised compilation examines the role of Dinacharya — the classical daily regimen — in preventing the health hazards posed by pollution, in the Department of Swasthavritta, Parul Institute of Ayurveda, Parul University. The work surveys the classical framework of Ahara (diet), Vihara (behaviour) and their rules of combination, place, time, quantity and quality, and then maps each of the major contemporary pollution categories — air, water, soil, noise, light, thermal and radioactive — against the specific protective and remedial measures that Dinacharya prescribes. Because classical texts address pollution-related illness as an accumulation of doshas disturbed by specific etiological factors, the compilation argues that preventive daily routine is a more realistic first line of defence than any downstream treatment, particularly in communities where environmental exposure cannot be immediately controlled. The work concludes that a structured, seasonally appropriate daily routine remains a clinically meaningful and accessible intervention for pollution-related morbidity.
